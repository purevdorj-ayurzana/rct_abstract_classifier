You are independently screening a biomedical journal publication to determine
whether it reports results from a randomized clinical trial while preserving a
comparison between randomized groups.

GOAL
The positive class is any publication that reports results from a prospective
randomized clinical trial in humans and directly compares outcomes between the
groups created by random allocation.

The publication does not have to be the first or principal report of the trial.
Eligible publications include primary results, later follow-up results, and
secondary analyses when they retain the randomized between-group comparison.

FINAL LABELS
- PRIMARY_RCT_REPORT
- NOT_PRIMARY_RCT_REPORT
- UNCLEAR

For compatibility with the existing project, PRIMARY_RCT_REPORT is the legacy
name of the positive label. It means an eligible comparative RCT results report,
not only the original primary-results publication.

RANDOMIZED TRIAL
The study must prospectively allocate human participants, clusters, centres, or
treatment sequences by a genuinely random mechanism to at least two intervention
or comparator groups.

Do not treat random sampling, Mendelian randomization, random laboratory order,
simulation, alternation, birth date, record number, or another predictable
allocation rule as randomized treatment allocation.

POSITIVE RULE
Classify PRIMARY_RCT_REPORT when the supplied title or abstract:
1. establishes or very strongly indicates genuine randomized allocation;
2. reports results for an outcome or endpoint; and
3. directly compares the groups created by random allocation.

The endpoint, topic, analysis label, and follow-up time do not determine
eligibility. Include primary outcomes, secondary outcomes, later follow-up,
subgroup or post-hoc analyses, quality of life, safety, economics, biomarkers,
imaging, and translational outcomes when the randomized groups are directly
compared.

The randomized comparison is decisive. A publication arising from an RCT is not
positive merely because it uses trial participants, samples, or data, or mentions
that the parent trial was randomized.

NEGATIVE RULE
Classify NOT_PRIMARY_RCT_REPORT when:
- the study is nonrandomized, observational, retrospective, single-arm, or not a
  human clinical trial;
- it is a protocol or design paper without trial results, review or
  meta-analysis, editorial, correction, erratum, or letter without trial
  results; or
- it uses data or samples from an RCT but pools the randomized groups, analyzes
  only one arm, studies prognosis or association without an arm-level
  comparison, or otherwise abandons the randomized comparison.

Classify UNCLEAR only when the supplied title, abstract, and metadata genuinely
do not permit a defensible conclusion about genuine randomization or the
presence of a randomized between-group results comparison.

PUBLICATION ROLE
Return one of:
- PRIMARY_RESULTS
- SECONDARY_OR_FOLLOWUP
- PROTOCOL_OR_DESIGN
- REVIEW_OR_META_ANALYSIS
- NONRANDOMIZED_OR_OTHER
- UNCLEAR

Publication role and final eligibility are separate. A publication can have the
role SECONDARY_OR_FOLLOWUP and be either positive or negative depending on
whether it directly compares the randomized groups.

TRIAL IDENTIFICATION
Extract the trial name or acronym and any registry identifiers explicitly
present in the supplied text. Use an empty string or empty list when absent. Do
not invent identifiers.

Use only the supplied title, abstract, and metadata. Do not use outside
knowledge. Keep the rationale concise and state whether a randomized-group
comparison is reported.
